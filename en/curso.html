Define a video you can make today

You can define the audience, action, and limit for a short video.

When your first idea tries to show everything, each scene creates another decision. A smaller scope lets you finish and learn.

In 1 minute

  1. Audience: neighbors looking for a break
  2. Full video: 15–20 seconds, divided into four shots
  3. Limit: no more than two tries per shot

1Scope: the size of your first deliverable

Scope is the limit of the work.

Our example is a 15- to 20-second invitation for the fictional Ponto da Pausa café.

The audience is neighbors looking for a quiet break.

The video will have four shots: a cup, a snack close-up, the café, and an invitation.

A shot is one continuous segment with no cut.

A simple video makes it easier to notice where the image changed or the message became unclear.

The 24-hour deadline is an organization challenge.

Account access, credits, generation queues, and review time may require another day.

Caio, a 29-year-old communications student, replaces the business’s whole story with a twenty-second invitation.

2Control before spectacle

A usable result depends on repeatable decisions: a recognizable product, similar lighting, and one action per shot.

Changing the setting, face, and camera at the same time increases what you need to check.

In this course, the sequence is reference, still image, motion, and edit.

Review each step as you go.

A flashy transition cannot fix a handle that disappeared.

Choosing what must stay the same first makes it easier to decide whether a generated result works.

Also set a limit on the number of tries before spending credits.

Helena, a 55-year-old café owner, keeps the same cup and setting so viewers recognize her offer.

A completed brief ends the planning stage; only a playable file proves the video was produced.

Before and after shows a change.

Product spotlight brings attention to an object.

Setting, detail, and invitation present a place.

Problem and solution focus on a specific difficulty.

A versus B compares two options using the same criterion.

These are communication structures, not formulas that guarantee reach.

The café has photos of its space and coffee.

Write four lines that become scenes

You can write a four-line script and time it.

A script full of adjectives may sound nice without showing anything. A short line with a possible image is easier to produce.

In 1 minute

  1. Opening: Need a break?
  2. Value: coffee, a snack, and a quiet corner
  3. Invitation: visit Ponto da Pausa

1Hook, value, and invitation

A hook is an opening that gives people a reason to keep watching.

It could be a specific question, a curious detail, or a familiar situation.

Our example: Need a break?

Then we show coffee and a snack, introduce the café, and invite people to visit.

Each line has a different job.

The opening should match what comes next.

Promising a surprise the video does not deliver breaks expectations.

The first three seconds are a planning limit for this exercise, not a guarantee that people will keep watching.

Caio replaces an abstract phrase with coffee and a snack, which he can show in one shot.

2One line, one possible image

Read the lines aloud and time them with your phone clock.

Cut repetition before speaking faster.

Next to each line, write what we will see.

A steaming cup can be filmed or generated.

An unforgettable experience still needs to be translated into a visible situation.

An AI assistant can suggest versions, but you must confirm the information.

Ask for four lines, an audience, and a duration.

Then review every word.

Do not accept an address, price, or hours invented by the assistant.

Helena reads the invitation aloud and pauses before the café name.

Prepare a reference that shows the product

You can photograph an object and check the reference.

A confusing starting image lets the generator fill in details you needed to preserve. The reference should show what matters.

In 1 minute

  1. Reference: a photo you took of the object
  2. Check: shape, color, and visible details
  3. File: 01-reference-original

1The object needs to be recognizable

A reference is an image used to guide appearance.

For a real product, start with a photo of your own.

Place the object near a window and choose a simple background.

Show the important parts without cropping the edges.

Check whether reflections, shadows, or fingers hide any details.

A clear photo does not guarantee fidelity, but it gives you something to compare.

If the handle is missing from the reference, it is hard to complain that the generator invented one.

Choosing the angle is part of being in control.

Caio clears the background before taking the photo and makes sure the handle is visible.

2The starting image is a scene decision

A starting image, also called a first frame, is the image from which the motion begins.

It can be a photo or a generated image that you have reviewed.

It should already show the framing you want.

For this exercise, use a vertical format and leave a small margin around the product.

A very tight composition may lose parts of the object when cropped.

Check it in your phone gallery before sending it.

Keep the original and a selected copy with an easy-to-recognize name.

Helena keeps the original photo to compare the cup’s color with later videos.

The object should fit in the needed frame, with its important details visible.

Lock the look before varying the scenes

You can record three fixed features and one allowed change.

A face or product that changes at every cut is distracting. A short reference sheet helps you recognize the same project across different scenes.

In 1 minute

  1. Fixed: cup color, wood, soft light
  2. Can change: close or wide shot
  3. Character: optional in this first video

1Continuity: making the same world recognizable

Continuity is the sense that scenes belong to the same project.

Color, clothing, objects, light, and setting help create that impression.

Choose three features to keep fixed.

Example: a teal-green mug, a wooden table, and window light.

Then state what may change, such as camera distance.

Without this distinction, you may approve attractive images that do not fit together.

The visual style should stay clear too.

Photography, drawing, and stylized animation look different.

Mixing them calls for an intention, not just whatever result appears.

Caio chooses photography with window light and saves a talking character for another project.

2A character is a choice, not a requirement

A product video can work without showing faces.

For this first project, hands or objects are enough.

If you include a character, use a fictional adult or someone who authorized use of their image.

Give them a working name and describe their clothes, hair, and role in the scene.

A name helps organize files; it does not guarantee the generator will preserve the person.

Always compare the images.

Skipping characters in your first video reduces the details you need to control and review.

Helena allows a wider shot but checks that the product remains recognizable.

A character’s or product’s name organizes the prompt; the image shows whether it was preserved.

Draw a four-scene storyboard

You can lay out four frames with an image, movement, and timing.

A script becomes a production plan when each line has an image and a place in the sequence. Simple sketches are enough to plan.

In 1 minute

  1. Order: mug → snack → setting → invitation
  2. Editing times: 4 + 4 + 5 + 5 = 18 seconds
  3. One action per shot

1Storyboard: the sequence before the video

A storyboard is a set of frames showing the scene order.

Draw four vertical rectangles on paper.

Represent the mug, snack, setting, and invitation inside them.

Use simple shapes.

Under each frame, write the matching line and approximate duration.

The sketch reveals whether two scenes repeat the same information.

It also lets you check whether the invitation has room for text.

You can still change the order without spending a video generation.

Caio divides eighteen seconds among the four frames before opening the generator.

2One idea per shot

A shot begins and ends before the next cut.

For a first attempt, choose one small action for each shot.

Rising steam, a camera move closer, or a hand adjusting the plate are different actions.

Combining them in one prompt increases the review work.

Divide fifteen to twenty seconds among the four shots.

These are editing targets: you can generate a longer clip and use only part of it.

Check the total and leave enough time to read the invitation.

Helena sets aside five seconds so the final invitation is readable.

Storyboard durations are editing targets. Check which lengths the generator offers before creating a clip.
